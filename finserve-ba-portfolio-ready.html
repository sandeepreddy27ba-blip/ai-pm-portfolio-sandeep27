Business Context

A US financial services company headquartered in New York with multiple branch offices faces three compounding operational failures: no structured borrower data capture system, manual and slow document processing, and no visibility into branch-level performance.

These failures compound each other — poor data capture leads to poor communication, which leads to client frustration, which converts to competitor churn. The operational blindspot at branch level means leadership cannot identify or course-correct these issues in time.

The system is designed as an integrated platform — not siloed tools — so that borrower data flows seamlessly from application through processing to decision, with all communication and analytics derived from the same data source.

Three Core Problems at a Glance

Each problem maps directly to a solution component in the proposed system.

Problem 1 — Borrower Data
No structured system to capture borrower details. Communication post-finance is ad hoc, inconsistent, and often non-existent — damaging relationship continuity and repeat business potential.
Solution — Borrower CRM + Communication Engine
Centralized borrower profile with lifecycle tracking. Automated event-triggered notifications (email + SMS) at every milestone. All communication logged against borrower record.
Problem 2 — Document Processing
Manual, slow document handling causes client frustration and churn to competitors. No visibility into which documents are missing, pending, or rejected at any point in the process.
Solution — Document Management System + Workflow Engine
Digital upload portal with format validation, virus scanning, classification, and checklist-driven status. SLA clocks on each case. Automated alerts for overdue items. Borrower gets specific, actionable feedback — not generic rejections.
Problem 3 — Branch Operations Visibility
HQ has no reliable system to track application volumes, processing times, conversion rates, or revenue targets by branch. Management is flying blind on performance.
Solution — Branch Operations Dashboard
Real-time dashboard for branch and regional managers. KPIs include: applications received, processed, converted; average cycle time; SLA breach rate; revenue vs. target. Refreshed every 15 minutes during business hours.

Detailed Problem–Solution Mapping

Each identified problem, its root causes, impact, and the corresponding IT solution recommendation.

Problem 1 — Borrower Data & Communication

Root cause: No CRM or structured data store for borrower profiles. Loan officers likely maintain spreadsheets or physical files — not searchable, not shareable, not automated.

Business impact: Borrowers who financed once are not re-contacted for refinancing, top-up loans, or referral programs. Lost lifetime value. No audit trail of interactions.

Solution: Centralized Borrower Management Module — persistent profile capturing identity, contact, employment, loan history, communication preferences. Communication Engine sends automated, personalized notifications at every milestone.

Borrower CRMCommunication EnginePhase 1
Problem 2 — Document Processing Delays

Root cause: Documents are likely submitted physically or via unmanaged email. Loan officers manually verify format and completeness. No systematic tracking of what is received vs. outstanding.

Business impact: Processing delays frustrate clients who have time-sensitive purchase decisions. They switch to competitors who offer faster turnaround. Reputational damage compounds over time.

Solution: Document Management System with digital upload, automated validation, classification, and checklist tracking. Loan Processing Workflow Engine with SLA timers and escalation alerts.

Document ManagementWorkflow EnginePhase 1
Problem 3 — Branch Operations Tracking

Root cause: Each branch operates in isolation with no standardized reporting structure. HQ cannot aggregate performance data in real time. Management visibility is delayed, inaccurate, or absent.

Business impact: Underperforming branches go unidentified. Best practices from high-performing branches are not replicated. Revenue forecasting is unreliable. Resource allocation is inefficient.

Solution: Branch Operations Dashboard — real-time aggregation of all application data by branch, region, and product line. Key metrics: volume, processing time, SLA compliance, conversion rate, disbursement value vs. target.

Phase 1: Report-based export for branch managers. Phase 2: Interactive real-time dashboard for HQ with drill-down to branch and loan officer level.

Clarifying Questions for Requirements Elicitation

These are the questions a BA must raise before locking requirements. Unanswered, they represent design risk. Answered, they sharpen solution accuracy.

Borrower Data & Communication
  • What communication channels do borrowers currently prefer — email, SMS, phone, or in-branch? Does this vary by age segment?
  • Does the company have a preferred CRM or marketing automation platform already in use, or is this greenfield?
  • Should the borrower portal be web-only, or is a mobile app expected within scope?
  • What languages must the system support? Is Spanish required given the US geography?
  • Should the system support co-applicants (e.g., joint home loans between spouses)?
Document Processing
  • What is the current average document processing turnaround time, and what is the desired SLA target?
  • Are documents reviewed manually today, or is there any existing OCR or extraction tool in use?
  • What regulatory requirements govern document retention — how long must originals be stored?
  • Which document types are mandatory vs. optional per loan product (home loan, personal loan, SME)?
  • Is e-signature acceptable for declarations and consent forms, or are wet signatures required?
Branch Operations
  • How many branch offices are there, and is data currently in spreadsheets, a legacy system, or not captured at all?
  • Is the HQ view required in real-time, or is a daily-refreshed dashboard acceptable for Phase 1?
  • Should branch staff use the same platform as the borrower, or is a completely separate internal tool needed?
  • Which branch-level KPIs does leadership currently track informally — to ensure the dashboard replaces rather than duplicates?
Business Context & Integrations
  • What loan products does the company offer? (Home, personal, auto, SME?) Each has distinct document and approval workflows.
  • What is the expected application volume per month, per branch?
  • Are third-party integrations anticipated — credit bureaus (Equifax/Experian/TransUnion), identity verification APIs (e.g., Jumio), e-signature services (DocuSign)?
  • Is there an existing core banking system the new IT platform must integrate with, or is this standalone?

Design Assumptions

Stated clearly to make design decisions transparent and allow stakeholders to challenge any assumption before development begins.

1
Loan Products
The company offers at least two loan products: personal loans and home loans. The system is designed to be configurable per product, with its own required document checklist and approval workflow.
2
Borrower Portal Scope
The portal will be web-based, desktop-primary, and mobile-responsive. A native mobile app is explicitly out of scope for the initial release.
3
CRM — Greenfield
The company does not currently have a functional CRM. The new system will own the borrower profile and communication history from scratch — no data migration from an existing system.
4
Document Review — Human-Led (Phase 1)
Document review remains human-led in Phase 1. OCR and automated data extraction are logged as Phase 2 enhancements — not blocking the initial release.
5
Communication Channels
Communication is triggered via email (primary) and SMS (secondary). Phone call scheduling is a manual process and out of system scope. WhatsApp and in-app push are Phase 2 considerations.
6
Dashboard Refresh Rate
The branch operations dashboard will show data refreshed at least every 15 minutes during business hours. True real-time streaming is a Phase 2 enhancement.
7
Regulatory Compliance
The system must comply with GLBA (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act) for data privacy and applicable fair lending regulations. HMDA reporting and CFPB-specific modules are Phase 2 scope.
8
User Roles — Phase 1
Three roles in Phase 1: Borrower, Loan Officer, Branch Manager. HQ analytics is a report export in Phase 1. Interactive HQ dashboard with drill-down is Phase 2.
9
Credit Bureau Integration
Integration with one major credit bureau (Experian) for automated credit score pull is in scope for Phase 1, subject to API availability and legal agreements. If unavailable, manual pull process is the fallback.
10
Infrastructure
The system will be deployed on a cloud platform (AWS or Azure) with no dependency on on-premise servers. The existing IT infrastructure is confirmed cloud-ready by the company.

Design rationale: Showing total steps and time estimates upfront is a deliberate UX decision. Research shows that unknown journey length is the primary cause of form abandonment. The borrower must know what they're committing to before they commit.

Main Success Scenario — Step by Step
1
Application Initiated — Reference Number Assigned
Borrower selects "Apply for a Home Loan." System immediately assigns Application Reference No. (e.g., APP-2024-10482) and displays it prominently. This gives the borrower tangible proof that something real has started — critical for trust.
2
Progress Stepper Displayed with Time Estimates
A 5-step progress bar with estimated time per step is shown at the top. Borrower can see the complete journey before committing. Reduces the cognitive fear of an unknown-length form.
3
Step 1 — Personal Information with Inline Validation
Borrower fills name, DOB, SSN, email, mobile, address, employment status, income. Inline validation fires on blur (field-by-field), not on final submit — errors corrected as they occur, not after a full-page failure message.
4
Credit Inquiry Transparency — SSN Tooltip
When the SSN field is reached, a tooltip appears: "We use this to verify your identity and pull your credit report. This is a soft inquiry and will not affect your credit score at this stage." This single line resolves the borrower's single largest implicit anxiety.
5
Save Progress — Resume-Link Email Sent
If borrower doesn't have all documents ready, they click "Save Progress." System auto-saves and sends a secure resume link via email. Session can be closed. Progress is not lost.
6
Step 3 — Document Upload with Specific Checklist
Dynamic checklist: uploaded items show green checkmark, outstanding items show amber, rejected items show red with specific reason (e.g., "Document is blurry — please re-upload a clearer scan"). Not generic "invalid document."
7
Application Submitted — Confirmation Screen + Assigned Officer
On-screen confirmation shows: reference number, list of submitted documents, estimated decision timeline (3–5 business days), and name and contact of the assigned loan officer. Email confirmation sent immediately. SMS at 24 hours: "Your application is with your officer. No action needed today."
Alternate Flows & Exception Handling
A1
Missing Document on Submission
System does not silently reject. It flags the specific document, shows exactly where to upload it, and allows the rest of the application to be submitted with that document logged as pending. Loan officer is notified to follow up proactively — borrower is not left to wonder.
A2
Session Timeout Warning
At the 25-minute mark, in-page warning appears: "Your session expires in 5 minutes. Save your progress now?" One click extends session. All progress entered is auto-saved regardless of whether the warning is seen.
A3
Borrower Drops Off Mid-Form
System triggers re-engagement email at 24 hours: personalized with borrower's name and the specific step they abandoned. Contains a direct deep-link back to that step. Not a generic "complete your application" blast.
A4
Credit Bureau API Unavailable
Application is accepted without credit pull. Loan officer is automatically flagged to initiate manual credit check within 24 hours. Borrower is not made aware — their experience is not degraded by a third-party outage.
A5
Document Fails Virus Scan
File is quarantined. Borrower receives a specific notification: "The file [filename] could not be processed for security reasons. Please re-upload the document from a different device." No technical jargon. Clear action required.

Persona & Pain Point Analysis

Deep-dive into the primary borrower persona — explicit and implicit pain points, emotional drivers, and design responses for each.

RS
Ravi Sharma, 34
Software Engineer · Houston, TX · First-time home loan applicant
Background

Ravi and his wife have been renting for six years and have finally saved enough for a down payment. He found FinServe via a Google search comparing rates. He is digitally comfortable but financially inexperienced with mortgages. He is comparing three lenders simultaneously — this company is not yet his first choice.

What Ravi Sees (Eyes)

He opens the portal on a Tuesday evening after his child is asleep. He immediately scans for: how long this will take, and whether the site looks secure and legitimate. He notices no visible security signal near the SSN field, no explanation of why SSN is needed, and no sense of how many pages he must complete. The form looks generic.

What Ravi Thinks (Mind)

He is building a mental scorecard against two competitor sites: "Is this trustworthy? Will I hear back? What if I make a mistake — can I fix it?" He does not know if filling out this form will trigger a hard credit inquiry. That fear alone may cause him to abandon — and he will never explain why.

What Ravi Feels (Heart)

Underneath the pragmatic checklist is the emotional weight of a life decision. This is his family's home. He wants to feel that the company he is trusting with his financial details is professional, organized, and will keep him informed. The moment the portal feels cold or opaque — he closes the tab.

Secondary Persona — Maria Chen, 58, Branch Loan Officer

Maria has been processing loan applications at the Houston branch for 12 years. She manages 20–30 active cases simultaneously using a combination of email, physical files, and a basic spreadsheet. She is competent but overwhelmed.

Explicit pain: "I spend half my day chasing clients for missing documents and the other half updating my spreadsheet. I have no time to actually process loans."

Implicit pain: She is afraid the new system will make her job harder, not easier. She has seen "digital transformation" projects that created more work. She needs to see immediate time-savings in the first week of use or she will revert to her spreadsheet.

Design implication: The workflow engine must reduce her administrative load visibly. Document checklist status should be auto-updated by borrower uploads — she should never have to manually chase a document that is already in the system.

Internal (Loan Officer & Branch) Flow
1
Application Lands in Queue
New application auto-assigned to loan officer based on branch, product type, and current caseload. SLA clock starts. Officer receives email alert.
2
Initial Review & Document Check
Officer reviews application data and document checklist. Outstanding items are flagged. System auto-notifies borrower for missing items — officer does not chase manually.
3
Credit Assessment
System pulls credit score via Experian API (Phase 1). If API unavailable, officer is flagged for manual pull within 24hrs. LTV ratio, DTI calculated automatically from income data entered.
4
Underwriting & Decision
Officer logs decision (Approved / Declined / Referred for senior review). Decision reasoning is recorded. Audit log captures every action with timestamp and officer ID.
5
Decision Communicated + Case Closed
Communication engine sends decision notification with next steps. Approved: disbursement process initiated. Declined: reason letter generated per fair lending guidelines. Case archived to audit log.
